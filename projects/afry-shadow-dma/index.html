Part 1: Company overview and value chain

1.1 About AFRY

AFRY AB is a Swedish engineering, design and consulting company with operations in 50+ countries and over 19,000 employees. It operates across five divisions: Energy, Process Industries, Infrastructure, Industrial and Digital Solutions, and Management Consulting. AFRY's stated mission is to accelerate the transition towards a sustainable society through engineering and advisory services.

AFRY is subject to CSRD reporting from the 2025 financial year onwards and conducted a Double Materiality Assessment in 2023-2024 as part of its ESRS compliance preparation.

1.3 Key business characteristics for the DMA

  • Asset-light professional services model: own environmental footprint is relatively small
  • High-impact downstream: AFRY's influence on sustainability outcomes flows primarily through client projects
  • Significant workforce exposure: 19,000+ employees across diverse geographies and sectors
  • Supply chain exposure: heavy reliance on subcontractors, especially for large infrastructure projects
  • Regulatory exposure: directly subject to CSRD (2025), EU Taxonomy, and upcoming CSDDD

Part 3: Materiality scoring

3.1 Scoring methodology

Each ESRS topic is scored on two dimensions using a 1-5 scale:

  • Impact materiality (1-5): severity and scale of AFRY's actual or potential impacts on people and the environment
  • Financial materiality (1-5): likelihood and magnitude of financial effects on AFRY from ESG risks and opportunities

Overall score = average of impact and financial materiality scores. Topics scoring 3+ on either dimension are considered material.

Scoring key: 1 Negligible · 2 Low · 3 Medium · 4 High · 5 Critical

3.3 Key findings

  • Most material: Climate Change (E1) and Own Workforce (S1), both scoring 5 on impact materiality. These are AFRY's core sustainability priorities.
  • Highly material: Business Conduct (G1), critical for a global professional services firm where reputation is the primary asset.
  • Emerging material: Biodiversity (E4) and Value Chain Workers (S2), driven by CSRD/CSDDD regulatory pressure and client project exposure.
  • Watch: Water (E3), currently low materiality for AFRY's business model but relevant for specific project types.

4.3 Priority recommendations

  • Immediate (2025 reporting): quantify Scope 3 emissions by category, the single largest gap for E1 compliance
  • Immediate (2025 reporting): publish quantified climate scenario analysis outputs per ESRS E1 requirements
  • Short-term (2025-2026): develop a supplier audit programme and disclose results for ESRS S2 and CSDDD readiness
  • Short-term: disclose the gender pay gap figure as required under ESRS S1
  • Medium-term: develop biodiversity KPIs for infrastructure project impacts (ESRS E4)

Part 5: Summary and analyst notes

5.1 Overall assessment

AFRY is at an advanced stage of sustainability maturity for a professional services firm. It has:

  • Completed a formal DMA under ESRS principles in 2023-2024
  • Published GRI-aligned reporting with limited assurance
  • Integrated TCFD recommendations and begun climate scenario analysis
  • Set quantitative sustainability targets across climate, workforce and ethics

However, full CSRD compliance from 2025 will require significant deepening of disclosures, particularly in Scope 3, biodiversity, supply chain due diligence, and quantified financial risk exposure.

5.2 Analyst notes

This shadow DMA was conducted using publicly available information only. A full commissioned DMA would additionally include:

  • Primary stakeholder interviews (employees, clients, suppliers, investors)
  • Internal financial data for quantified risk and opportunity sizing
  • Site-level environmental data for project impact assessment
  • Legal review of CSDDD and EU Taxonomy applicability
